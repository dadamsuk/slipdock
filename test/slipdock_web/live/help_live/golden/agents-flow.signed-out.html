How work flows

What happens to a card on a board that runners work, from the moment you write it to the deploy: who acts at each stage, what the board checks, and where it waits for you.

The other help pages say how to connect an agent. This one says what it does once connected. Each check below is a board setting, off until you turn it on; with them all off, an agent's done is simply done.

At each stage the board posts the card's step status itself, as an On track status update by Slipdock: it shows at the top of the card and in its Status updates, and, being routine, tells nobody following the card. Agents needn't post these; a person's own update (at risk, off track) still tells them. A finished card shows its outcome there instead of a next step: done when, accepted by whom, merged and shipped.

1. Writing the card

Who acts: Person

The card's description is the ask : what you want and why. Its bar says what done means, one line per part, and is what the work is judged against. On a board with A bar before work starts on, an agent can't start a card without one ( slipdock edit 12 --bar "…" ).

A person acts here: Write a bar that can be met. An agent may not take a step that is yours: changing a handbook rule, merging, deploying to production, applying a proposal. Phrase those as what an agent can do: "proposed as a garden proposal (rule id, wording)" rather than "W-12 has rule Y", and "pull request open, its CI green" rather than "merged". A bar asking for a person's step is caught at pickup and comes back to you as a wait.

On a board with Lanes on, give the card its paths : the files its work will touch, as globs ( slipdock edit 12 --paths "lib/billing/**,docs/manual.md" ). Two agents can't work cards whose paths overlap at once; the second waits. Set lanes to Advise ( slipdock set-board B --lane-mode advise ) and the second starts anyway, its job's prompt naming the card already in those files. Files nearly every card edits, such as the manual, go in the board's shared paths ( slipdock set-board B --shared-paths docs/manual.md,README.md ): cards can declare them without holding each other, and their edits are left to merge.

An epic planned with the plan skill can start with spikes : a question only building can answer, which you confirm, becomes a card with the spike profile ( slipdock add 12 "Spike: does the API page?" --profile spike ), its bar the question. A spike needs no paths and holds no lane, and its code is never merged: its answer is a page pinned to the epic. Planning waits until the last is accepted, then a product wait on the epic asks you to resume, and the Spec is written from the answers.

On a board with a handbook (its Handbook/ wiki pages), the card lists the sections the builder should read first, and the rules that cover its paths ( slipdock card 12 ).

2. Queued and claimed

Who acts: The board Builder

Step status: Writing code Reworking after verify failed

An automation rule queues a job for a pool : Send cards to a runner when a card arrives in a list, or Work a list with a runner , which sends the top card, one at a time. A runner in that pool pulls the job, or a session takes it with slipdock claim-job B --pool loop .

Recurring agent work (a nightly sweep, the handbook's reconcile) lands in the board's Scheduled list, when it has one: a list in the scheduled category, after To Do. Nobody picks from it, people or agents asked what to do next. Its own Work Scheduled with a runner rules send each card on its day: when a list becomes Scheduled, every rule working the ready list gets one beside it, for the same pool. A Scheduled card still there after its due day is on its owner's Waiting on you .

Small fixes and investigations that sweeps, findings triage and agents file wait in the Housekeeping list (category housekeeping ). It gets the same rules beside the ready list's, Work Housekeeping with a runner , but each one backfills: it sends a card only while To Do and Scheduled have nothing that pool could start. Your own work always goes first. A Housekeeping card that turns out to need a design choice, or grows into feature work, comes back to you: the agent moves it to Triage with a product wait saying what grew, and builds nothing. With no Triage list on the board, it stays where it is, held by its wait until you answer. With the overseer on, each morning (07:00 unless you set another time) its card gets a digest of what Housekeeping worked, grouped by theme, naming only what needs you.

The card moves into progress, and the bar locks : from then on only a person can change it. For a Work a list with a runner job the server makes the move the moment a runner claims it, to the board's doing list (the one the guide names), and the job's prompt names that list; for any other job the agent moves the card. A job is held, saying why, while the board is paused, its WIP limit is full ( WIP limits hold automated work ), another card is in progress, or its lane overlaps one.

While the job is claimed or running, the card's Assignees show the loop : the runner and the job's kind (build or verify), linked to the job. It goes when the job ends. The agent doesn't assign the card to anyone, since it signs in with a person's token.

A person acts here: A batched board releases on a trigger, not on a merge. A card there is done when it merges; agents don't wait for its release. A release runs when a critical card merges, at the board's age cap, or when the owner presses Release now ( slipdock release B ): a runner's release job runs the board's release command, one release at a time. A command other than the default gh workflow run runs only on a machine whose local.json lists it under release_commands . A red release holds the next one and files a critical fix card: fix that card, and its merge lifts the hold and releases. When the cause is outside the repository, complete it --no-code : the fix card accepted with no code lifts the hold and releases nothing, and the held batch goes at the next trigger. A deploy that succeeds at the held sha or past it (the failed run rerun) lifts it too; only the owner lifts a hold by hand. Not shipped counts from the release there, never from the merge, so a card awaiting release is not late.
A person acts here: A pause is yours to lift. Agents may pause runner work ( slipdock pause B --reason "…" ), and an agent that sees it takes nothing. Only a person resumes it: slipdock resume B or the Runners panel.

3. Building

Who acts: Builder

Step status: Waiting for human

The builder reads the card and its handoff, and checks each part of the bar can be met. It keeps to the handbook's rules for the files it touches ( slipdock handbook B --paths lib/a.ex,lib/b.ex ): breaking a must rule gets the card rejected, a should is noted. It runs the project's own pre-push checks, the ones its CLAUDE.md or contributing guide lists, and comments on the card as it goes.

Everything it writes on the card is Markdown, and the card shows it that way: the description, bar, evidence, comments, handoffs, questions and status updates. It is told to write each as a list rather than one dense paragraph, so you can skim it.

A person acts here: Answer its waits. Nobody watches a job, so an agent that needs something only you can give (a decision, a credential, a bar it can't meet) opens a wait and stops. The job is parked and the card goes back on top of its list. Answer it on the card, in My work's Waiting on you ( slipdock work ), or with slipdock answer 12 "…" , and the card is sent again with your answer at the top of its prompt, to whichever runner of the pool is free. A wait is for what is yours to decide: readings that differ in cost or in what you see, product, UX, security and release calls, and your steps (merging, a handbook rule, a deploy). A bar item a later decision made moot isn't one: the agent reads it the obvious way, says so on the card with the citation, and the verifier judges it. A question sets out each option and what it would mean, and recommends one. A wait that offers choices names the one the agent proposes, so your answer can be one click; with An approval's proposal stands after set, an unanswered one of kind other goes ahead with it and you are told. A wait that has gone moot (answered elsewhere, the bar edited) is withdrawn by the next agent to find it, with a comment saying why.

4. Shipping

Who acts: Builder CI

Ship accepted work by chooses how the work reaches the default branch, and so the deploy.

  • By push (the default): the builder pushes the default branch. If a push to main deploys, the work is live before anyone has checked it.
  • By pull request : the builder never pushes the default branch. It pushes a card-12 branch and opens a pull request. That pull request's CI is the one full test run: the builder runs only the tests it changed and the pre-push checks first, then watches the checks with gh pr checks card-12 --watch --fail-fast , so a failure is seen when it happens. A card that changes no code (a sweep, a handbook reconcile) has no pull request: it completes with slipdock done 12 --no-code "why" , and accepting it merges nothing.

5. Submitting

Who acts: Builder

Step status: Verifying Waiting for human to accept

The builder completes the card with its evidence : what it checked, the commit, the tests it added or changed, the suite's result, and the pull request ( slipdock done 12 --evidence-commit abc1234 --suite passed:412:0 --pr URL --evidence - , the rest on stdin). With Evidence on completion on, a completion without any is refused. Evidence that is a bare - is refused on any board: it is what a client that doesn't read stdin sends for --evidence - , so pass the text itself, or pipe it from a CLI that reads stdin. A card's description or bar that is a bare - is refused the same way, on a create or an update, for --desc - and --bar - .

Evidence and a reason are lines, not a paragraph : a first line that stands alone, a blank line, then one short line per point, the parts in --check or the evidence's fields rather than repeated in the text. The command line loses line breaks, so they go on stdin ( --evidence - , --reason - ): 

slipdock done 12 --evidence-commit abc1234 --suite passed:412:0 --evidence - <<'EOF'
Built and tested; CI green.

- the export keeps archived cards (`Export.run/1`)
- Handbook: updated W-3 §Exports
EOF

On a board with a handbook, the closing comment has a Handbook: line , the sections it updated or why none needed it, and a Findings: block : what the card taught that the code and handbook don't show, for the daily triage to handle. An item already dealt with says where and is kept promoted, not open: [done: W-1950 §Local setup] after its kind, or → #2087 at its end for a card filed for it. A fix is a card, not a finding: a concrete change someone could be handed (a missing test, a stale doc, a tooling bug) goes on the board as a card at once, in the Housekeeping list when the board has one (a design question goes to Triage, for a person), and the block names it with the arrow; a finding is only for what is uncertain, cuts across cards, or shows the handbook wrong.

A person acts here: No verifier, no check. With A second agent verifies off, the agent's completion is final: the card is done and in Done. Read the evidence yourself if you want it looked at.

6. Verifying

Who acts: Verifier Person

Step status: Verify failed; queued for rework Waiting for human (rejection limit) Waiting for human (held before merge) Accepted; merging Merged Done

With A second agent verifies on, the completion is a submission : the card stays in progress, awaiting verification. With Runner pool for verify jobs set, a verifier in that pool, never the runner that built it, judges in order: does the bar cover the ask; is each part of the bar met; did the suite pass (on a pull request board, the pull request's checks); are the rules kept ( must and should ; a rule broken only in code the change didn't touch is reported as pre-existing and kept as a finding, never a rejection); and is the Handbook line honest. A rejection names every instance found: once a rule is broken, the verifier looks for every other instance of the same break (the same list, the same string, the same door) and lists them all under that one rule, each with its file and line, so one round fixes them all. The rule that a change is written up everywhere a reader would look ( SPEC-10 ) is checked by enumeration: for a card in its scope the verifier greps the names the diff introduces or alters across the seven places and gives each one updated, deferred to a card whose bar names that place, not applicable with why, or missing, and one missing is the rule broken. With profiles on, a docs card gets no verifier (the server accepts it on its suite), and a schema or security card's verifier also reviews the diff for defects, rejecting on an Important one. On a fix , feature , schema or security card the verifier also mutates the change: it breaks 3 to 5 of the lines the change added or changed and, on top of those, every guard or branch the change adds (a new condition, clause, pattern match or early return), each named with its line, one at a time, runs only the card's tests and restores each line. A break no test catches (a surviving mutant) is a rejection naming the mutation and the test to write; on a fix, one break undoes the fix, which the regression test must catch. A survivor in lines the change didn't touch is kept as a finding. The builder runs the mutation review before submitting, on its own change, and names the mutants it tried in the evidence, one line each ( mutant:killed=lib/foo.ex:42:flip > to >= ): that list is a map, not a verdict: the verifier carries a killed one only when it broke it again or a test the builder names plainly covers that line, and checks every other claim in the evidence too (a named test is read and run, a docs place is opened). The profile is checked against the change the agent completes with ( slipdock done 12 --diff-base origin/main ): a migration makes it schema , a security path security , and over 400 changed lines a fix or docs card a feature , never lower. Then slipdock verify 12 accept or slipdock verify 12 reject --reason - , the reason on stdin as lines, its parts in --check : 

slipdock verify 12 reject --check bar:2=fail:"no test" --reason - <<'EOF'
bar 2: the export drops archived cards

- `Export.run/1` filters on `archived_at`
- no test exports an archived card
EOF

On a bug, the ask is the reported symptom : the check is would the reporter now see it fixed, not does the work meet the bar. The builder reproduces the reported symptom on the reporter's scenario and posts the repro as its own comment; a clean repro that doesn't show it opens a wait saying what was seen and asking for a screenshot or steps (which screen, which control), never conditions built until something fails. The verifier rejects ( the ask: … ) a bug card whose card or evidence says the reported symptom or its cause was never seen, however good the tests.

  • Accepted by push: the card is done. By pull request: a runner merges it, and the merge completes the card. A failed merge comes back as a rejection, unless the pull request was already merged: at the accepted commit the card completes with that merge, at another head it stays merging and says which head, and neither counts as a rejection.
  • Rejected : the card goes back to the top of its list, and the reason is the next pass's brief. Submitted again, it is judged on what failed and what the new commits touch; a part that passed before and nothing since touches is carried, shown as carried from the commit it passed at.
  • Audits check the checker : with Audit 1 in N accepted cards set, another runner rechecks about one accepted card in N. The acceptance stands; a disagreement files the rework as a card in the ready list, titled from the audit's reason, with what failed and a link to the audit, caused by the accepted card (high priority for a surviving mutant or a broken must rule). The alert names it and clears itself once that card is accepted or completed, and a second disagreement comments on it rather than filing another. The verify prompt then lists the board's last five audit misses of the last 30 days, each its rework's title and the parts it failed, for the verifier to check first; the audit prompt never does.
A person acts here: You decide when no verifier can.
  • With no verify pool, or no runner in it other than the builder's, the card waits for you: Accept or Reject on the card, or slipdock accept 12 . It is in My work's Waiting on you .
  • A second rejection, or one for a rule an earlier rejection named, says the card is going round in circles: it stays flagged review until it is accepted, a comment lists the rounds and what failed in each, an alert goes up and you are emailed, and it heads My work's Waiting on you . The runners still rework it; look at whether it needs a different approach or a reworded bar.
  • After Rejections before a person decides (2 by default), the next rejection opens a wait on the board's owner: rework it as rejected, or accept it as is. It lists what failed each time, and asks whether the approach, not the fix, is wrong. You can reword the bar first.
  • When no runner can merge, merge the pull request yourself and press Merged it myself ( slipdock verify 12 merged ).
  • An audit that disagrees has already filed its rework card: read the alert, and reword or reprioritise the card if the audit was wrong about what matters. The alert goes by itself when the rework is accepted or completed.

7. Afterwards

Who acts: CI Person

Step status: Shipped to production Reopened

Your CI deploys what reaches the default branch. With delivery metrics on, it records the deploy ( slipdock deploy run --env production -- ./deploy.sh ), and the cards it names get a Shipped line. A merged card no deploy names after 20 minutes (the board's Not shipped after ) shows an amber Merged 1a634c6 · not shipped after 45m instead and raises a warning alert, overseer on or off, until a deploy names it: not_shipped in the card's JSON and get_card , Not shipped in slipdock card . On a board with batched releases a merge deploys nothing: the card is done, shows merged, awaiting release , and the 20 minutes count from its release ( release_requested_at in not_shipped ), not from its merge.

The handbook improves from use . The nightly Reconcile the handbook card brings its pages up to date with what landed, and Triage findings merges repeated findings and promotes what recurs, filing a new rule or decision as a proposal for you.

A person acts here: The proposals are yours to apply.
  • A rule is a person's call. An agent proposes one ( slipdock garden propose B proposal.json ), and you apply or dismiss it on the board's Tidy-up page ( Tidy up… in the board's menu, or slipdock garden B ). A rule an agent edited itself shows there too, for you to keep or put back.
  • Findings are handled by the daily triage and the server. A finding seen three times (the board's setting) is drafted as a rule on the Tidy-up page, and those proposals are what wait for you: the board's Findings page (a tile on the board's Agentic Building page, counting them; a card with open findings links to it) and slipdock findings B lead with them. A finding a proposal names waits on your decision, out of the open list triage works from ( --status waiting ). The findings below are reference, to promote, merge or dismiss by hand when you want ( slipdock finding 41 promote --note "…" ).
  • What shipped is on the board's Ledger page (a tile on Agentic Building ), or slipdock ledger B [--since 7] : since 07:00 yesterday, each card with who accepted it, its evidence, the should rules it missed, where its time went and what its jobs spent (the model's and the tools' time, and tokens where the logs carried them, the new ones beside the cache reads). Skim it each morning, and open any card's chat from it. A card a runner worked shows the same under Lead time in its panel, phase by phase.

The settings, in one place

Each is on the board's Agentic Building page: turn on Agentic Building in Board settings, Features, then open it from the board menu. From a terminal, slipdock set-board sets each. A card's subcards take their parent board's choices.

A bar before work starts
Agents write each card's bar before they may start it; it locks then.
slipdock set-board B --bar-required
Lanes
Hold: an agent can't start a card whose paths overlap a card in progress. Advise: it starts, and its prompt names that card.
slipdock set-board B --lane-mode hold
Profiles
A card an agent starts gets one word for what kind of change it is; a schema or security card an agent accepts waits for a person before it merges, and a spike merges nothing and holds no lane. A docs card gets no verify job, and a schema or security card's verifier also reviews it for defects. A fix, feature, schema or security card's verifier breaks a few of the changed lines to see that a test fails.
slipdock set-board B --profiles on
Human tier, by profile
Change what an agent's acceptance of a profile does: none, ledger or before_merge.
slipdock set-board B --profile-tier schema=none
Shared paths
Files nearly every card edits: an overlap there never holds a card or a job.
slipdock set-board B --shared-paths docs/manual.md,README.md
WIP limits hold automated work
A full first in-progress list holds runner jobs too.
slipdock set-board B --automation-wip
Evidence on completion
An agent completing a card says what it checked.
slipdock set-board B --evidence-required
A second agent verifies
An agent's completion is a submission, decided by someone else.
slipdock set-board B --verify-work
Runner pool for verify jobs
Sends each submission to that pool as a verify job. Blank: you decide.
slipdock set-board B --verify-pool checkers
Ship accepted work by
Push (the default) or pull request. Pull request needs verification on.
slipdock set-board B --ship-via pull-request
Rejections before a person decides
The rejection after this many opens a wait on the board's owner (2; 0 never).
slipdock set-board B --verify-rejection-limit 2
Audit 1 in N accepted cards
Another runner rechecks about one accepted card in N (0, the default, none).
slipdock set-board B --verify-audit 10
An approval's proposal stands after
An unanswered approval wait (kind other, with a proposed choice) goes ahead with its proposal (off by default).
slipdock set-board B --wait-default-after 4h

The overseer: when to look

The board's owner can turn on an eye on outcomes, in the board's Automations panel under Overseer , or with slipdock overseer B --on . Every minute the server checks the tree for work going round (a second rejection, a third runner pass), something waiting on you while nothing runs, a card past the board's P85 or a job on one step for 30 minutes, idle runners beside a full ready list or jobs queued behind one lane, and an epic whose open subcards are all blocked.

One check needs no switch: on a board with delivery metrics on, a card merged more than 20 minutes ago (the board's Not shipped after , slipdock set-board B --not-shipped-minutes N ) that no succeeded deploy names raises an alert whether or not the overseer is on. On a board with batched releases the minutes count from the card's release, not its merge.

Each fires once: an alert, an email, and the top of My work's Waiting on you , saying why and what to do. It clears itself when the condition ends. A cheap model on one of your runners (a job_overseer in its config) reads only those and the pulse, never a card or a log, and writes a digest on the board's Overseer card: every 10 minutes while cards are active, longer when little or nothing is in flight. It flags; it never acts.
