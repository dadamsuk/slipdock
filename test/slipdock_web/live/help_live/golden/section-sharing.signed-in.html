Sharing

Share a board, a single card, a saved view, or a wiki page or folder with a person or a group. Give each one read-only, comment or edit access.

Access levels

Every board has one owner : the person who made it. Anything you share, you share at one of three levels:

  • Read only : can see, but not change anything.
  • Can comment : a reader who can also comment, post status updates and vote, but not change the card itself.
  • Can edit : can change cards and pages.

Only the owner can rename or delete a board. The server enforces these levels, and they apply the same way to the API, the CLI and agents.

What you can share

  • A board , from its settings: covers every card on it and every sub-board inside them. A board shared with you sits on your board list, marked with who shared it.
  • A single card , from its window: the person sees that card and nothing else on the board. It's listed under "Cards shared with you" on their home page.
  • A saved view , from the Views menu: the person sees the board only through that view, and only the cards it selects.
  • A wiki page or folder : see below.

Wiki pages and folders

Share… on a page's or folder's menu shares it with someone who has no reason to see the rest of the board. A folder shares everything filed in it, to any depth, and a page brings the images and files it embeds. What's shared with you appears under Shared with me , below your wiki tree, without showing the board it's on.

Someone with edit access to a shared folder can write and rearrange inside it, but can't delete anything, move it out of the share, or share it on.

Groups and new people

Groups are named sets of people you can share with in one go. Sharing with an email address that has no account usually invites that person. Whether that's allowed, and who may register at all, is up to the server's admin.

Signing in needs no password: you get a one-time link and code by email, or you can use a passkey once you've added one.

Public links

For readers without an account, a saved view or a wiki page can be published at a public, read-only link. A published view is live; a published page is its text plus a snapshot of its query answers. Unpublishing withdraws the link immediately.
